Hi, I am Nunzio Perta.

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow in the [De Rubeis Lab](https://labs.icahn.mssm.edu/derubeislab/), led by Dr. Silvia De Rubeis, at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. My research aims to understand how small changes in proteins can alter brain development. I focus on DDX3X, a protein that helps cells control how RNA is used, and on DDX3X syndrome, a rare genetic disorder that affects brain development. I investigate why changes in DDX3X can affect females and males differently, andd how they alter the growth and shape of neurons. I am also studying how DDX3X helps RNA and proteins gather into temporary structures inside cells and how these structures influence neuronal development.

Previously, I completed my Ph.D. in Biomolecular Science at [Marche Polytechnic University](https://www.univpm.it/Entra/) in Italy under the mentorship of Prof. Daniele Di Marino. My doctoral research bridged computational biology and experimental science. I studied how a protein’s sequence shapes its structure, movement, interactions, and biological function. I combined computer modeling and simulations with biochemical and cell-based experiments. The biological systems varied, but the guiding question remained the same: which molecular change explains the biological effect that we observe?

Several projects deepened my interest in neuroscience. I modeled a membrane protein complex that helps neurons keep their membranes properly organized and designed [PI8a2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-026-09097-y), a small protein-like molecule used to study what happens when this balance is lost. I investigated how [FOXP1 variants](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c00080) interfere with DNA binding and contribute to a neurodevelopmental disorder. I also studied JNK3, a protein involved in neuronal damage, and developed [SIMBA2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41419-026-09244-5), a new molecule designed to selectively block JNK3 and protect neurons after stroke. These projects taught me that computer-based ideas are most valuable when they are designed to be tested experimentally.

During the final phase of my Ph.D., I joined the De Rubeis Lab to expand my training in brain development and human genetics. This experience allowed me to apply my background in protein biology to neurons and shaped my current research direction. My long-term goal is to establish an independent laboratory focused on understanding how disease-associated changes in proteins disrupt brain development and on developing strategies that can be tested in relevant neuronal and animal models.

I hold an M.S. in Molecular and Applied Biology and a B.S. in Biological Sciences from Marche Polytechnic University. I have also had the opportunity to meet families affected by DDX3X syndrome. Their questions have shaped how I think about my research and remind me that its purpose is not only to explain a molecular mechanism, but also to produce knowledge that matters to people living with the disorder.

## Research Interests

Molecular Neuroscience, Brain Development, Neurodevelopmental Disorders, DDX3X and DDX3Y Biology, RNA and Protein Organization in Cells, Protein Structure and Motion, Computational Biology, Protein and Peptide Design
